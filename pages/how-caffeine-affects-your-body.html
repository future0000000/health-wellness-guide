How Caffeine Affects Your Body

A look at how caffeine works, how long its effects last, and how to think about your personal intake.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

Caffeine is one of the most widely consumed psychoactive substances in the world, and understanding how it actually works can help you make more informed choices about your intake.

How caffeine works

Caffeine blocks adenosine, a brain chemical that builds up throughout the day and promotes feelings of sleepiness. By blocking it, caffeine creates a temporary sense of alertness rather than adding new energy.

How long caffeine stays in your system

Caffeine has an average half-life of about 5-6 hours in healthy adults, meaning half of the amount consumed is still in your system that many hours later, which is why afternoon coffee can interfere with nighttime sleep.

General intake guidelines

Health authorities in several countries commonly cite around 400mg of caffeine per day (roughly 4 cups of brewed coffee) as a general upper limit for most healthy adults, though individual tolerance varies considerably.

Signs you might be consuming too much

Jitteriness, a racing heart, difficulty sleeping, and increased anxiety can all be signs that your current caffeine intake exceeds what your body handles comfortably.

Caffeine tolerance and withdrawal

Regular consumption leads to tolerance, requiring more caffeine over time for the same effect, and abruptly stopping can cause temporary withdrawal symptoms like headaches and fatigue, which typically resolve within a few days.
