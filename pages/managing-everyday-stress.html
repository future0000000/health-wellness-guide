Managing Everyday Stress

Evidence-informed strategies for managing everyday stress, from breathing techniques to lifestyle adjustments.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

Some stress is a normal part of life, but chronic, unmanaged stress can affect both mental and physical health over time, making it worth building tools to handle it.

Breathing techniques

Slow, deep breathing activates the body's relaxation response. A simple technique is inhaling for 4 seconds, holding for 4 seconds, and exhaling for 6-8 seconds, repeated for a few minutes.

Physical activity as a stress outlet

Exercise helps metabolize stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline, and even short bouts of movement, like a 10-minute walk, can noticeably reduce feelings of tension.

The role of sleep and stress

Stress and poor sleep often feed into each other: stress makes it harder to sleep well, and poor sleep makes it harder to cope with stress, so addressing one often helps the other.

Setting boundaries

Chronic overcommitment is a common source of ongoing stress. Learning to say no to nonessential demands on your time protects the capacity you have for what matters most.

When stress becomes overwhelming

If stress starts interfering significantly with daily functioning, relationships, or physical health, speaking with a mental health professional can provide tools and support beyond self-management strategies.
