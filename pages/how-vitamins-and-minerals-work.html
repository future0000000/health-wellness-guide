How Vitamins and Minerals Work

A clear explanation of what vitamins and minerals do, the difference between them, and how deficiencies happen.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

Vitamins and minerals are often grouped together as "micronutrients," but they're chemically distinct and play different roles in keeping the body functioning properly.

What vitamins are

Vitamins are organic compounds the body needs in small amounts for processes like energy production, immune function, and cell repair. Most vitamins can't be produced by the body in sufficient amounts and must come from food.

What minerals are

Minerals are inorganic elements, like calcium, iron, and potassium, that support functions including bone structure, oxygen transport, and nerve signaling.

Fat-soluble vs water-soluble vitamins

Vitamins A, D, E, and K are fat-soluble and get stored in body fat, meaning excessive intake can potentially build up to harmful levels. Water-soluble vitamins, like vitamin C and the B vitamins, aren't stored as readily and excess amounts are typically excreted.

How deficiencies develop

Deficiencies can develop from inadequate dietary intake, certain medical conditions that affect absorption, restrictive diets, or increased needs during specific life stages like pregnancy.

Do most people need supplements

For people eating a varied, balanced diet, many nutritional needs can be met through food alone; supplements are generally most useful for addressing a specific identified deficiency or need, ideally guided by a healthcare provider rather than taken generically.
