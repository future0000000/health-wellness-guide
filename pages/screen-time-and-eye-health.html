Screen Time and Eye Health

How extended screen use affects your eyes and practical habits to reduce digital eye strain.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

With screens now a near-constant part of daily life, understanding how extended use affects eye health has become increasingly relevant.

What is digital eye strain

Digital eye strain refers to a group of symptoms including dryness, blurred vision, headaches, and neck or shoulder discomfort associated with prolonged screen use.

Why screens cause more strain than reading

People tend to blink less frequently while looking at screens, which reduces the natural moisture layer on the eye's surface and contributes to dryness and irritation over extended periods.

The 20-20-20 rule

A commonly recommended strategy is looking at something roughly 20 feet away for 20 seconds, every 20 minutes, to give the eye muscles focused on close-up screen work a brief periodic rest.

Adjusting your environment

Positioning screens at eye level or slightly below, reducing glare from overhead lighting, and adjusting screen brightness to match the surrounding room can all reduce strain during extended use.

When to see an eye doctor

Persistent symptoms despite adjusting habits, sudden changes in vision, or eye pain that doesn't improve with rest are reasons to schedule an eye exam rather than continuing to self-manage.
