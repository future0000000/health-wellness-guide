Understanding Your Blood Pressure Numbers

What the two numbers in a blood pressure reading mean and what ranges are generally considered healthy.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

A blood pressure reading includes two numbers, and understanding what each one measures helps make sense of what your doctor is telling you.

Systolic vs diastolic pressure

The top number, systolic pressure, measures the force of blood against artery walls when the heart beats. The bottom number, diastolic pressure, measures that force when the heart is resting between beats.

General reference ranges

Normal blood pressure is generally considered to be below 120/80 mmHg. Readings consistently at or above 130/80 mmHg are often classified as elevated or high, though exact thresholds and categories can vary slightly between health organizations.

Why high blood pressure matters

Chronically high blood pressure forces the heart and blood vessels to work harder than they should, which over time increases the risk of heart disease, stroke, and kidney problems.

Factors that influence blood pressure

Diet (particularly sodium intake), physical activity level, stress, sleep quality, and genetics all play a role in blood pressure levels, which is why lifestyle changes are often a first step in management.

When to seek medical advice

A single high reading isn't necessarily cause for alarm, since blood pressure fluctuates throughout the day. Consistently elevated readings over multiple occasions warrant a conversation with a healthcare provider.
