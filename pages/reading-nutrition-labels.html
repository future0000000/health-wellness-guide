How to Read a Nutrition Label

A practical guide to understanding nutrition labels, serving sizes, and which numbers actually matter most.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

Nutrition labels contain a lot of information, and knowing what to focus on makes them far more useful for making informed food choices.

Start with the serving size

All the numbers on a nutrition label are based on a specific serving size, which is sometimes smaller than what people typically eat in one sitting. Checking the serving size first prevents underestimating your actual intake.

Percent Daily Value (%DV)

This column shows roughly how much a nutrient in one serving contributes to a general daily diet. As a rough guide, 5% or less is generally considered low, while 20% or more is generally considered high for that nutrient.

Nutrients to generally look for less of

Added sugars, sodium, and saturated fat are nutrients most people benefit from moderating, since excessive intake of these is commonly associated with various long-term health concerns.

Nutrients to generally look for more of

Fiber, protein, and certain vitamins and minerals are nutrients most people benefit from getting more of, particularly since typical diets often fall short of recommended fiber intake specifically.

Watch the ingredient list too

Ingredients are listed by weight, from most to least. A short ingredient list with recognizable items is often (though not always) associated with less processed foods compared to long lists with many additives.
