Posture and Back Pain

What research actually says about posture and back pain, beyond the common assumptions.

This article is for general educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for guidance specific to your situation.

"Fix your posture" is common advice for back pain, but the actual relationship between posture and pain is more nuanced than the simple cause-and-effect story often assumed.

Posture alone rarely explains chronic pain

Research has generally found weaker links between static posture and chronic back pain than commonly believed; many people with "poor" posture have no pain, while others with seemingly good posture do experience it.

Movement matters more than any single position

Staying in any single position, good or bad, for extended periods tends to contribute more to discomfort than the specific posture itself. Regular movement and position changes throughout the day appear to matter significantly.

Core and back strength

Weak core and back muscles can reduce the body's ability to support the spine comfortably during daily activities, which is part of why strengthening exercises are commonly included in back pain management plans.

Ergonomic adjustments that can help

Adjusting chair height, monitor position, and keyboard placement to reduce strain, combined with regular breaks to stand and move, are practical steps that can reduce discomfort during long periods of desk work.

When back pain needs medical attention

Pain that radiates down a leg, is accompanied by numbness or weakness, or doesn't improve with normal self-care over a couple of weeks warrants evaluation by a healthcare provider.
